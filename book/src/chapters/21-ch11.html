Chapter 11

The Heist Structure

A heist runs in five phases: the briefing, the plan, the job, the scramble, and the debrief where everyone agrees it went fine and no one mentions the cat.

Phase 1 — The Score

The Storyteller presents the job. What’s the target, where is it, what’s the location? The ST lays out the map — the grid the crew will be working on — and describes what a spider can actually see from the entry point: the geography, the obvious features, the threats in plain sight.

What the ST does not do is read out a list of everything that will go wrong. The crew sees what’s visible. What’s hidden stays hidden until they find it — by casing the place, or the hard way.

This phase is roleplay. No rolls, no Alert. Just the job, and the crew deciding whether to take it. (They take it. Let them deliberate anyway.)

Phase 2 — Planning

Now the crew turns what they can see into a plan. Two things happen here: Casing and Preparation.

Casing — earning intel. The crew can dig for information they don’t already have. Each spider may take one Casing roll — Perception or Tactics, or a relevant Perk or ability — and each Success reveals one concrete piece of intel the ST is holding: a threat and its rough patrol line, a locked square, a gap in a wall, a hazard, the type of lock on the case. Lookouts and their Perks shine here. What the crew doesn’t uncover, they walk in not knowing. Intel is earned, not handed out — and it’s information, not a free win. Knowing the cat’s route doesn’t beat the cat. It just means you’re not surprised by it.

Preparation — setting up, not skipping ahead. The crew may also make a few concrete preparations on the known map. Each is tactical setup, not a guaranteed success:

  • Choose your entry square, or split the crew across two entry points.
  • Pre-place one Silk Line on a route you know.
  • Stash one small item in a known square (see the Ghost’s Dead Drop).
  • Set a Contingency, a Shortcut, or a Trap, if a Perk grants one.

For each Preparation, the ST designs one complication — not one that cancels the prep, one that adds texture. The window is real, but the world is complicated, and preparation has limits.

Nothing in Planning auto-beats an obstacle — except the Lookout’s Contingency Perk, which buys exactly one pre-arranged success. Planning buys you knowledge and position. What you do with them is the heist.

Phase 3 — The Heist (3–4 Obstacles)

The crew moves. The Alert starts at 0 and begins to climb. Between the entry and the objective stand 3–4 obstacles — specific challenges, each with a clear goal, a success outcome, and a failure outcome.

Obstacles aren’t scripted scenes. They’re situations the crew solves however they like, on the grid, with the dice and the Alert handling the consequences. At least one obstacle per heist should be something the crew didn’t uncover in Planning — the thing casing missed, or the thing that wasn’t there yet.

Phase 4 — The Escape (1–2 Obstacles)

Objective or not, getting out is its own problem, and the Wheelman earns their keep. The Alert carries over unchanged — if the heist ended in Full Alert, the escape starts in Full Alert. That’s the job.

Escape obstacles are leaner than heist obstacles — about pace and pressure. The goal is always the same: get out. The route is negotiable.

Crucially, escape is not just an Athletics problem. Every spider needs a way to contribute. The Face can talk a staffer into looking the wrong way. The Tinkerer can jam a door behind the crew. The Ghost can hold an exit open. The Grifter can pull all eyes to the wrong side of the building. The Wheelman knows the route — but everyone has a part in reaching it. This is a design requirement, not a nicety: at least one escape obstacle per heist must be solvable without Athletics. A low-BODY spider with nothing to do during the escape means the ST didn’t build the escape right.

Phase 5 — The Debrief

The crew is out and mostly alive. Count what they got.

OutcomeAdvancement Points
Full SuccessEscaped with the complete objective. Full AP for the difficulty: Easy 2, Standard 3, Hard 5, Absurd 6, Legendary 8.
Partial SuccessEscaped alive but without the objective, or with incomplete loot. Half AP, rounded down.
LossThe whole crew went Out before escaping. (Replacements arrive at the next obstacle, so a Loss means every spider is Out at the same time.) No AP. Significant embarrassment. Not discussed.

Spending AP

1 AP: +1 to a Skill · 2 AP: +1 to an Attribute · 3 AP: a new Perk from your Role list (no maximum). After creation, Skills and Attributes can reach 5 (Attributes include the species bonus); the creation cap of 3 no longer applies. You start every heist with WIT + NERVE Silk Points, using your current WIT and NERVE.

Win: escape with the objective. Partial win: escape without it, everybody lives. Loss: the entire crew goes Out before anyone gets clear. Loss is rare, and when it happens it’s usually the funniest session the group has had.
