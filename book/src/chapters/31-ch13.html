Chapter 13

Running the Heist

Running Heisty Spideys is a little different from running most games, and the difference is worth getting straight up front.

You are not running a balanced encounter. You are not challenging the crew optimally, or protecting a plot, or keeping narrative control. You are running a place — a real location with real geography, threats that follow real routines, and at least one thing the crew didn’t account for — and you are running it honestly. Make the location feel real, the stakes feel earned, and the failures land funny. The crew does the rest.

Three Ways to Run It Well

1. Be a fair world, not a fair opponent. The cat patrols on its actual route. The human wakes because there was actually a loud noise. The vacuum turns because vacuums turn. You’re not trying to catch the crew — you’re being the place they broke into. The place catches spiders on its own.

2. Make failure a door, not a wall. When the crew fails — and they will — turn it into a new situation, not a stop sign. The lock doesn’t open and something shifts in the kitchen. “You failed, nothing happens, try again” is the least interesting outcome in any game. Every failure should hand the crew a new problem worth reacting to.

3. Say yes, then set a Difficulty. The crew will try things you didn’t plan for. The Grifter will impersonate a receipt. The Bruiser will attempt to intimidate a vacuum. The Face will try to have a real conversation with a goldfish. Let them. Pick a Difficulty, roll, and find out what happens. “Yes, roll for it” is almost always a better answer than “no.”

The Unknown Obstacle

Every heist should have at least one obstacle the crew didn’t uncover in Planning — not an unfair trap, but something that emerges from the location and the rising Alert. It’s not you ambushing them. It’s the world being a world. Introduce it when it becomes unavoidable — when the crew reaches the relevant spot, when the Alert crosses a threshold that makes it inevitable, or simply when the timing is right. Don’t foreshadow it in the Score. Let it arrive.

Pacing With the Alert

The Alert is your pacing tool. At low Alert, play the world calm. At mid Alert, foreshadow and put things in motion. At high Alert, escalate for real. At the Limit, don’t just announce the heist is over — play the lockdown. The human hits the lights. The cat goes into full hunt. A second vacuum appears from somewhere. The crew scrambles for the exits. This is the best part of the night; treat it that way.
