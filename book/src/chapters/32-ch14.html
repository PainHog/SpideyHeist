Chapter 14

Building a Heist Location

A good location is three things: a clear grid the crew can read, threats with real patterns, and at least two routes between any two points that matter.

Draw the Map

Build the location as a gridded map — squares the crew can count on. It doesn’t need to be to scale or beautiful; it needs to be legible. A typical location is 4–6 connected areas. A single room runs roughly 8–14 squares across. For each area, note:

  • Walls and doors — what blocks movement and line of sight, and where the gaps are.
  • Furniture and clutter — what gives cover, what counts as rough ground, what a spider can climb or hide behind.
  • Entry points — at least two ways in, ideally more.
  • The objective’s square — where the prize actually is.
  • One threat or hazard that lives in that area.

Then place your threat tokens where they actually start, and give each a patrol line measured in squares. The crew is allowed to count the distance to the cat. Let them.

Two Routes, Always

The single most important rule of location design: between any two significant points, there must be at least two viable routes. One over the counter, one along the baseboard. One through the vent, one under the door. A location with a single path isn’t a heist — it’s a corridor, and the crew is just waiting for the dice to fail. Give them choices and the location runs itself.

Active Threats

Every good location has things that move — the cat, the vacuum, the human up for water at 2 a.m., the guard spider. Each active threat should have:

  • A patrol pattern or routine, even an irregular one, mapped in squares.
  • A trigger — what flips it from passive to active.
  • An Alert reaction — what it does when the Alert hits certain thresholds.

Patrol Example — House Cat

Route: asleep on the living-room couch ~80% of the time. When bored or hungry, walks a loop through the kitchen and hallway — about 12 squares, one lap every few rounds. Trigger: noise above a threshold (the Alert reaching 3) wakes it and makes it curious. Alert 3: opens its eyes, turns toward the sound. Alert 5: up and walking toward the noise. Alert 7: actively hunting. While it hunts, Stealth near it is at the Lockdown penalty like everything else.

Setting the Alert Limit

When you build the location, set its Alert Limit from the table in Chapter 9: Easy 10, Standard 8, Hard 6, Absurd 4, Legendary 2. The Limit is the difficulty. A lower Limit means the location tolerates less before it locks down — that’s the whole knob. A first-time crew should start at Easy or Standard. A crew that cleared a kitchen and wants a pet store next is ready for Standard or Hard.
