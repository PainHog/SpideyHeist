Chapter 7

Building Your Spider

Character creation takes about ten minutes. Six if you’ve done it before. Forty-five if the table gets into an argument about which species is best. Do the steps in order. Random tables for every step are in Chapter 20 if you’d rather let fate decide.

Step 1 — Choose Your Species

Pick one from Chapter 4. This gives you a passive ability, an Attribute bonus, and your Speed. Some species pair naturally with certain Roles — a Wolf Spider Bruiser, an Orb Weaver Tinkerer — but there are no wrong picks. Play the spider that interests you. The crew wants variety, not optimization.

Step 2 — Choose Your Role

Pick one from Chapter 5. This sets your job, your Signature Move, and your bonus skill points.

Step 3 — Distribute Attributes

You have 10 points to spread across BODY, WIT, NERVE, and GRACE. Minimum 1 in each. No Attribute may exceed 5, including your species bonus. Spend your points, add your species bonus, done.

A 1 in an Attribute is a real limitation — a BODY 1 spider struggles with physical work and getting away — and that’s fine. A Tinkerer can’t outrun a cat; that’s what the Wheelman is for. Build your spider knowing their gaps, and build the crew to cover them.

Example Spread

Filament is a Jumping Spider Ghost. She assigns BODY 2, WIT 2, NERVE 3, GRACE 3 (10 points). Her Jumping Spider bonus adds +1 BODY and +1 GRACE, for final stats BODY 3, WIT 2, NERVE 3, GRACE 4. Quiet, quick, and slippery, with enough BODY to make the jump she’ll absolutely mention afterward.

Step 4 — Note Your Speed

Your Speed comes from your species (Chapter 4): most spiders 5, Wolf Spiders 7, Jumping Spiders 6, Crab Spiders 4. Write it on your sheet. It’s how many squares you move on your turn.

Step 5 — Distribute Skills

You have 12 points for Skills. Maximum 3 in any one Skill at creation — this cap includes your Role bonus points. Your Role adds 3 more points that must go on your two core skills, and can’t push either above 3.

You don’t need points in everything. Gaps are the reason the crew needs each other. Overlap is fine too — two spiders with Tactics means the plan survives one of you ending up in a jar.

Step 6 — Choose 2 Perks

Pick two from your Role’s Perk list. These are what make your spider different from every other spider of the same Role. Choose by playstyle, by what the crew is missing, and by which description made you laugh.

Step 7 — Choose Your Flaw

Take exactly one Flaw — either choose one from the list, or roll 1d10 for a random one. One flaw, either way. It’s a real constraint, and it will come up.

When your Flaw causes a genuine problem for the crew, you earn 1 Silk Point. If a Flaw would fire at a truly catastrophic moment — stacking with Lockdown penalties and a failed roll all at once — any spider may spend 1 SP to push it back one round. The Flaw still fires. You just get one beat to breathe.

Step 8 — Calculate Starting Silk Points

Your starting Silk Points = WIT + NERVE (after species bonuses). Write it down. It’s your most important resource, and you will spend it unwisely. Everyone does.

Step 9 — Name Your Spider

Spider names tend toward objects they’ve been near (Pebbles, Dusty, Jar Lid), things humans call them (Bathroom Spider, The One in the Corner), plain titles (Eight-Legs, The Boss), or names significantly more dramatic than the spider has earned (Shadowfang, Lord of the Upper Shelf). There’s a name table in Chapter 20. One of the entries is just “Gerald.” You are allowed to be Gerald.
