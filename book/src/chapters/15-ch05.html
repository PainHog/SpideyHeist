Chapter 5

Crew Roles

Every heist crew runs on specialists. Heisty Spideys has seven — more than Ocean’s Eleven per head, and considerably more legs.

Your Role is your job on the crew. It grants bonus points in your core skills, a Signature Move no other Role has, and a list of Perks. You pick two Perks at creation.

The Face

“Relax. I’ve got this. No — actually, keep worrying, that part’s fair.”

The Face is the crew’s social specialist. Where others use force or stealth or engineering, the Face uses conversation — with cats, humans, guard spiders, whatever stands between the crew and the door. Charming, quick, and occasionally making things worse in ways that won’t surface until it’s far too late to bring up.

Core Skills: Deception, Persuasion · Role Bonus: +3 points split between them.

Signature Move — “That’s Not What Happened”

Once per heist, after any roll — including one the ST made for an NPC — spend 2 Silk Points to reframe what just happened in the crew’s favor. You don’t change the mechanical result; you change the story around it. The cat didn’t spot you — it spotted a bit of fluff. The human didn’t hear a crash — the building settled. Deliver it smoothly, with total confidence. The ST accepts it. If the cat rolled to pounce, the pounce still happens — you’re just explaining why it landed on nothing important.

PerkEffect
Silver TongueWhen you fail a Persuasion roll, reroll it once. You said it better the second time.
Read the RoomBefore a social encounter, ask the ST one thing the NPC actually wants. They answer honestly.
Plausible DeniabilityOnce per scene, when the crew does something that would raise the Alert by 1, talk that single point away. Stops a +1 only — never a +2 or higher. Not an undo button for the loud mistakes.
Fast TalkDistract any NPC as a free action, no roll, once per scene. It holds for exactly one round.
Actually, I Planned ThisOnce per heist, after a plan visibly falls apart, declare it was the plan. The crew gets +1 die on their next roll as everyone commits to the lie.
Familiar FaceYou’ve been here before. You know one true detail about the target or location that wasn’t in the briefing.

The Ghost

“I was never here. That shadow wasn’t moving. Go back to sleep.”

The Ghost lives in the spaces between spaces — vents, wall gaps, the three squares behind the fridge nobody has cleaned since 2018. Where others work around security, the Ghost simply isn’t there when it looks.

Core Skills: Stealth, Acrobatics · Role Bonus: +3 points split between them.

Signature Move — “Phase Through”

Once per scene, move through any space a spider could theoretically fit — a crack, a pipe, the gap under a door — no roll, no Alert increase, regardless of the current Alert Level. You arrive on the far side the same turn. Use it to get into a room, out of a room, or, in one documented case, away from a cat so fast the cat sat down and pretended it had never cared.

PerkEffect
Silk TrailLeave a near-invisible line anywhere you’ve been. Crew can follow it or ride it as a zip line. Holds two spiders.
Dead DropStash a small item anywhere during Planning, no roll. You know exactly where it is. Nobody else does.
SoundlessReduce the Difficulty of Stealth rolls made while moving by 1. You don’t make noise. You make a point of it.
Second-Story SpiderClimb any surface at full Speed with no roll — even slick or hostile ones that would cost other spiders double or a check.
Ghost ProtocolWhen you’re the one spotted, immediately make a free Stealth roll to vanish again before the NPC reacts.
I Was Never HereIncrease the Difficulty for any NPC to remember seeing you by 2. You are deeply, fundamentally forgettable.

The Tinkerer

“I can build that. I need a paperclip, some lint, and forty minutes. …Thirty. Give me thirty.”

The Tinkerer is the crew’s engineer, gadget-maker, and lock-cracker, and the reason there’s always something strange in the bag. They build tools out of what’s lying around, and the tools work about as often as tools made of lint and crumbs should — which is more often than you’d think.

Core Skills: Engineering, Perception · Role Bonus: +3 points split between them.

Signature Move — “I Made a Thing”

Once per scene, as your Action, cobble a one-use gadget out of materials at hand — anything plausibly on the map. Pick one effect:

  • Bypass: defeat one small mechanical obstacle within reach — jam a sensor, pop a simple latch, wedge a drawer — no roll.
  • Boost: hand it to a crewmate for +2 dice on their next roll.
  • Distraction: set it off in a target square within 6, pulling every nearby NPC’s attention there for one round.

The ST can veto materials that clearly aren’t available. When in doubt, they’ll ask what you’re building it out of.

PerkEffect
Jury-RigOnce per scene, fix, jam, or modify a small mechanical device with a Difficulty 2 Engineering roll. Locks, sensors, that one drawer.
Spider-Sense… Sort OfSpend a turn observing an area and the ST tells you whether any mechanical traps, alarms, or sensors are present — not where.
OverclockSpend 1 Silk Point for +2 dice on an Engineering roll. It just takes more legs than usual.
Field RepairOnce per scene, patch an adjacent crewmate’s Vitality up one level with a Difficulty 2 Engineering roll. You’re an engineer, not a doctor. Close enough.
Trap ArchitectDuring Planning, set one web-based trap on the known map. When an NPC triggers it, the ST rolls Difficulty 3 for them to avoid it.
I See How This WorksThe first time you meet any new lock, alarm, or mechanism, ask the ST one yes/no question about how it works. Free. Just by looking.

The Bruiser

“The plan was to be quiet. That was before the plan involved the cat.”

The Bruiser solves problems physically — not for lack of other options, but because most problems, given enough spider at enough speed, resolve. First responder for anything involving direct confrontation, sudden escalation, or hauling something too heavy for everyone else.

Core Skills: Brawl, Endurance · Role Bonus: +3 points split between them.

Signature Move — “Make a Scene”

When the Bruiser causes or joins a significant physical commotion, they may pull every NPC in the scene onto themselves for one full round. This costs nothing — it’s simply what just happened. During that round, every other crew member in the scene gets +2 dice on any roll. Afterward, the entire room is looking very hard at the Bruiser, and the Alert goes up by 1. Worth it? Almost always.

PerkEffect
Take the HitOnce per scene, redirect a physical consequence meant for an adjacent crewmate to yourself. You chose this.
UnfazedIgnore the first −1 die penalty from being Rattled. You’ve been Rattled before. You’re fine.
Thunderous EntranceEnter a scene from an unexpected direction and every NPC must pass a Difficulty 3 check or freeze in surprise for a round.
Negotiating PositionYour presence helps. The Face gets +1 die on Persuasion while you’re visibly nearby.
Silk GrappleFire a silk line to grab and restrain a target up to 3 squares away — Brawl, Difficulty 3. Small targets held one round.
That All You Got?After a hit that would drop you to Critical, roll Endurance (Difficulty 3). On a success, you stop at Hurt instead.

The Lookout

“Cat at two o’clock. Human moving. Vacuum — the vacuum is doing the thing again.”

The Lookout is the crew’s eyes. They track every moving piece — patrol lines, human habits, the cat’s mood, where the vacuum is in its route — and relay it in real time. A good Lookout means nobody is surprised. A bad one means everybody is surprised at once, which is at least fair.

Core Skills: Perception, Tactics · Role Bonus: +3 points split between them.

Signature Move — “I Called It”

Once per heist, before any roll, declare you predicted exactly this during your surveillance, and say in one sentence what tipped you off. If it’s remotely plausible, the whole crew gets +1 die on that roll. If it’s genuinely plausible and fits what’s been established, +2 instead. If it contradicts known facts, you get nothing, and the ST is allowed to look at you for a while.

PerkEffect
Early WarningAt the start of each obstacle, the ST tells you which direction any active threat moves next. No roll.
Tactical FeedOnce per round, give a crewmate +1 die on their next roll by telling them exactly what to do. You’re not helping. You’re instructing.
Pattern RecognitionAfter watching a patrol or recurring threat for one full round, you know its next three actions.
ContingencyOnce per heist during Planning, set one Contingency with the ST. If a specific trigger happens, it activates automatically as a success.
Escape RoutesYou always know the fastest way out of a room you’ve been in. Reduce the Difficulty of escape rolls by 1 while you lead.
Counter-SurveillanceDetect when an NPC is watching a specific area — Perception, Difficulty 2. On a success, the ST tells you what’s watched and from where.

The Wheelman

“The exit is that way. It was always that way. We practiced this. WHY ARE YOU GOING THAT WAY.”

The Wheelman runs the getaway. They know every exit, the fastest line to the outside, and how to get eight legs through a closing window in under two seconds. The most important spider on the crew for the last five minutes of any job — which is also when everything is on fire.

Core Skills: Acrobatics, Tactics · Role Bonus: +3 points split between them.

Signature Move — “I Know a Way”

Once per heist, during the Escape only, declare a route nobody knew existed — a gap behind the washing machine, a vent straight to the outside, a window that’s mysteriously always cracked. Describe it; the crew can use it. It reduces the Difficulty of every Escape roll by 1 for the whole crew. The route has to be physically possible for spiders.

PerkEffect
Always a Way OutYou can’t be fully cornered. Even when the ST would logically have you trapped, you have one option left. It may be unpleasant.
ShortcutDuring Planning, establish one shortcut between two points on the known map. The crew can use it once.
Don’t Look DownRemove the penalty from height-based Acrobatics rolls. You’ve looked down. You’ve made your peace.
PassengerCarry one incapacitated crewmate at full Speed. It looks extremely dramatic.
DraftingWhen you lead group movement, each crewmate gets +1 die on movement rolls. You set the pace; they follow.
Abort, AbortOnce per heist, call an immediate abort on a failed plan. The crew retreats without the extra Alert that failure would have added.

The Grifter

“I am not a spider. I am a dust bunny. Please don’t look directly at me.”

The Grifter works through identity and misdirection. Where the Face talks through a situation, the Grifter changes what the situation looks like — disguises, false trails, planted information. They shape what everyone else thinks is happening, which is usually unrelated to what’s happening. On purpose.

Core Skills: Deception, Disguise · Role Bonus: +3 points split between them.

Signature Move — “You’re Looking at the Wrong Spider”

Once per heist, stage a Decoy — a fake version of what the crew is doing, somewhere NPCs can see it. Describe it, and every NPC in the location goes to investigate it for two full rounds, unless they pass a Difficulty 3 Perception roll. For those two rounds the real crew works unobserved, and every roll that’s part of the actual job gets +1 die.

PerkEffect
Method ActorChoose one NPC before the heist; you’ve studied them. +2 dice on all Deception and Disguise involving that NPC.
Double BluffWhen an NPC sees through your disguise, reveal the disguise was the distraction. Spend 1 Silk Point; the NPC is confused for a round.
Planted EvidenceDuring Planning, place one false piece of information on the known map. The first NPC to find it believes it completely.
Quick ChangeAlter your disguise between scenes with no roll. You look different now. You are still a spider.
The Long ConSpend Planning establishing a false identity and all rolls using it get +1 die. The identity must be specific and named.
Smoke and MirrorsWhen you fail a Deception roll, reframe the failure as intentional. The NPC still isn’t fooled, but they don’t raise the Alert.
