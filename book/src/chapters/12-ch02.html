Chapter 2

Core Rules — How to Roll Dice

When a spider tries something uncertain — a lock, a sleeping cat, convincing a human the scratching in the wall is nothing — you roll dice. Six-sided dice. Only six-sided dice.

The Core Mechanic

Roll a handful of d6s. Every die showing 4, 5, or 6 is a Success. The Storyteller says how many Successes the task needs. You get them or you don’t. That is the entire engine. Everything after this is detail.

The One Rule

Roll your pool of d6s. Count every 4, 5, or 6 as a Success. Get enough and the thing happens. Fall short and it doesn’t — and something else goes wrong. The dice do not care about your plan. The dice do not care about anything.

Building Your Dice Pool

Your pool = the relevant Attribute + Skill. The ST tells you which apply. Add them, grab that many d6s, roll.

Example

Pebbles, a Wolf Spider Bruiser, wants to shove a stuck cabinet door. The ST calls for BODY + Athletics. Pebbles has BODY 4 and Athletics 3, so she rolls 7 dice against a Difficulty 3 door. She rolls five Successes and flings the door open hard enough to bounce it off the wall. She succeeded. The heist may not have. The human is awake now.

Types of Results

Full Success — it worked. You got enough Successes. The thing happens. Enjoy it. The Alert is still right there.

Partial Success — it worked, but. At least one Success, fewer than needed. You make progress and a complication lands. The lock opens, but you made noise. You clear the gap, but you drop the bag. The ST picks the complication — ideally the funniest one that also actually costs you something.

Failure — it did not work. Zero Successes. No progress, and something gets worse. Failure is a beat, not a wall. The door stays shut and there’s a noise now. Every failure should cost something and reveal something.

Critical Success — you did it perfectly. Double the required Successes (6 on a Difficulty 3, 8 on a Difficulty 4). It works better than planned — you get the item and spot a second way out — and the Alert drops by 1 (only on a roll of Difficulty 2 or higher). This is the only thing that lowers the Alert during a heist. Savor it.

The Botch. Your pool has been reduced to 0 or below — by Vitality penalties, Lockdown, or a situation so bad the ST rules you can barely function (crushed against a surface, total darkness, upside down in a jar). You still roll one die. On a 1–3, Botch: you fail and the Alert jumps by 2. On a 4–6, a clean failure, no bonus disaster. This time.

A Botch is what happens when the Bruiser attempts subtlety at Stealth 0 while Critical. Once the table recovers, it is very funny.

Assists

Any crew member in the same scene who isn’t the one rolling can Assist, as their Action, if they can actually reach or help the active spider — usually meaning adjacent on the grid, or connected by a silk line, or otherwise plausibly lending a hand. They roll their relevant Skill only — no Attribute — to a maximum of 3 dice, minimum 1. Each Success adds one die to the active spider’s pool. Only one spider can Assist a given roll.

Opposed Rolls

When you act against something that’s actively resisting — an alert cat, a suspicious human, a vacuum in pursuit — both sides roll. Most Successes wins. Ties go to the defender. When you and the world tie, the world wins; it’s bigger than you. (The exception is taking a hit, where you are the defender — see Chapter 10.) Assists work normally, but only the spider side can receive one.

When Not to Roll

Not everything needs dice. If success is guaranteed, just say yes. A Stealth 4 spider crossing an empty kitchen at 3 a.m. walks across. The same spider crossing while the cat stares directly at it rolls.

Roll when the outcome is uncertain and failure would be interesting. If either is false, skip the dice. The story doesn’t need a d6’s permission to continue.

Designer’s Note

The most common table mistake is rolling for everything. Don’t. Rolls create tension, and tension is a resource — spend it where failure changes the story. Roll for every footstep and the Alert hits its limit before anyone finds the safe.
