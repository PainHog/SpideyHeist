Chapter 2

Core Rules — How to Roll Dice

When a spider tries something uncertain — a lock, a sleeping cat, convincing a human the scratching in the wall is nothing — you roll dice. Six-sided dice. Only six-sided dice.

The Core Mechanic

Roll a handful of d6s. Every die showing 5 or 6 is a Success. The Storyteller says how many Successes the task needs. You get them or you don’t. That is the entire engine. Everything after this is detail.

The One Rule

Roll your pool of d6s. Count every 5 or 6 as a Success. Get enough and the thing happens. Fall short and it doesn’t — and something else goes wrong. The dice do not care about your plan. The dice do not care about anything.

Building Your Dice Pool

Your pool = the relevant Attribute + Skill. The ST tells you which apply. Add them, grab that many d6s, roll.

Example

Pebbles, a Wolf Spider Bruiser, wants to shove a stuck cabinet door. The ST calls for BODY + Athletics. Pebbles has BODY 4 and Athletics 3, so she rolls 7 dice against a Difficulty 3 door. She rolls three Successes and flings the door open hard enough to bounce it off the wall. She succeeded. The heist may not have. The human is awake now.

Types of Results

Full Success — it worked. You got enough Successes. The thing happens. Enjoy it. The Alert is still right there.

Partial Success — it worked, but. At least half the Successes you need (round up), but fewer than all of them. You make progress and a complication lands. The lock opens, but you made noise. You clear the gap, but you drop the bag. The ST picks the complication — ideally the funniest one that also actually costs you something. By default it’s noise: the Alert rises by 1. The ST may swap that for a complication that costs as much — a dropped item, −1 die on your next roll, a hit from a threat that’s engaged — but never both.

Failure — it did not work. Fewer than half the Successes you need (at Difficulty 1–2, zero). No progress, and something gets worse. Failure is a beat, not a wall. The door stays shut and there’s a noise now. Every failure should cost something and reveal something. You may try again next round, and an engaged threat that sees you fail lands a hit (Chapter 10). If the crew is still stuck after five rounds at one obstacle, the ST ends it: in the heist the objective slips out of reach; in the Escape, anyone still inside is caught.

Critical Success — you did it perfectly. Double the required Successes (6 on a Difficulty 3, 8 on a Difficulty 4). It works better than planned — you get the item and spot a second way out — and the Alert drops by 1 (only on a roll of Difficulty 3 or higher). This is the only thing that lowers the Alert during a heist. Savor it.

The Botch. Your pool has been reduced to 0 or below — by Vitality penalties or a situation so bad the ST rules you can barely function (crushed against a surface, total darkness, upside down in a jar). You still roll one die. On a 1–3, Botch: you fail and the Alert jumps by 2. On a 4–6, a clean failure, no bonus disaster. This time.

A Botch is what happens when the Bruiser attempts subtlety at Stealth 0 while Critical. Once the table recovers, it is very funny.

Assists

Any crew member in the same scene who isn’t the one rolling can Assist, as their Action, if they can actually reach or help the active spider — usually meaning adjacent on the grid, or connected by a silk line, or otherwise plausibly lending a hand. They roll their relevant Skill only — no Attribute — to a maximum of 3 dice, minimum 1. Each Success adds one die to the active spider’s pool. Only one spider can Assist a given roll.

However many helpers and abilities pile in, a roll gains at most +2 bonus dice from Assists, Perks, Signature Moves and intel together. Silk Point dice (Overclock’s included) don’t count toward that limit.

Group Checks. When the whole crew must get past the same thing — sneak across a room, climb a cabinet, run for the vent — every spider rolls, and each one gets through on its own result. The round raises the Alert only once, by the worst result among those rolls, and a Critical lowers it only if every roll was a Critical.

Opposed Rolls

When you act against a creature that’s actively resisting — an alert cat, a guard spider, a vacuum in pursuit — it rolls its pool first, and its Successes + 1 become your Difficulty. (A tie still goes to the world; it’s bigger than you.) Then every Difficulty modifier applies as usual — cover, the Alert band, Perks, Flaws — and your roll reads Partial, Success or Critical like any other. Humans and anything else without a stat block use their Chapter 15 Difficulty. Assists work normally, but only the spider side can receive one.

When Not to Roll

Not everything needs dice. If success is guaranteed, just say yes. A Stealth 4 spider crossing an empty kitchen at 3 a.m. walks across. The same spider crossing while the cat stares directly at it rolls.

Roll when the outcome is uncertain and failure would be interesting. If either is false, skip the dice. The story doesn’t need a d6’s permission to continue.

Designer’s Note

The most common table mistake is rolling for everything. Don’t. Rolls create tension, and tension is a resource — spend it where failure changes the story. Roll for every footstep and the Alert hits its limit before anyone finds the safe.
