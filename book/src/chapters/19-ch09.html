Chapter 9

The Alert

The Alert is a single number that tracks how awake the location is. It starts at 0 and climbs toward a Limit the Storyteller sets before the heist. When it reaches the Limit, the place goes to Full Alert: the objective is lost, and the only goal left is getting out with your legs attached.

The Alert only goes up during a heist. The one exception is a Critical Success, which drops it by 1. Everything else is upward pressure. Plan accordingly.

The Alert is the location’s overall state of alertness, not any one creature’s senses. When Stealth gets harder at Alert 3, the cat didn’t suddenly grow better ears — the cat is awake and moving, covering ground it wasn’t before. Higher Alert means more things are active, in more places. The fiction justifies the number; the number just keeps score.

What Raises the Alert

AmountTrigger
+1A roll fails with a consequence — noise, attention, evidence.
+1Spotted briefly — an NPC notices something’s off but isn’t sure.
+1A Silk Clutch is used. The universe keeps score.
+2A Botch. Everything that could go wrong did, plus one new thing.
+2A confirmed alert — an NPC knows something is happening, even if not what.
+2A spider goes Out. The location notices something is very wrong.
+2A Loud Failure — something loud breaks, a crash that carries across the location.

Alert Thresholds

LevelWhat Happens
0–2 — CalmThe plan is working. Enjoy it. It won’t last.
3–4 — StirringSomething feels off. Stealth rolls have their Difficulty increased by 1.
5–6 — ActiveA threat has woken up — the cat is on its feet, a human checked the kitchen, the vacuum changed its route. No roll penalty yet.
7+ — LockdownEverything is wrong. All rolls have their Difficulty increased by 1. Multiple NPCs are active.
At Limit — Full AlertThe location is compromised. If the crew doesn’t have the objective yet, it’s out of reach. Escape is the only play, and it will be memorable. If they do have it, they just need to get out.

Storyteller Note — Two Thresholds People Confuse

Penalties stack. At Alert 7+, Stealth is +2 Difficulty total (the Stirring penalty and the Lockdown penalty both apply). Track them separately so the crew always knows what they’re facing.

The critical distinction: Active (5–6) turns threats on — it does not raise Difficulty. The cat wakes; the human stirs; nothing gets mechanically harder to roll yet. Only Lockdown (7+) raises Difficulty. This is the single most misread rule in the game. Active means something is now moving. Lockdown means everything is now harder.

The Alert should feel like pressure, not punishment. At 3, foreshadow — a creak, one open eye. At 5, escalate — make something active and specific. At 7, hard but survivable. At the Limit, the heist is over but the escape is its own story. Never just say “the Alert hit 8, you lose.” Make the lockdown land.
