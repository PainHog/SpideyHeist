Chapter 19

Five Ready-to-Run Heists

Each heist is ready to run with minimal prep — a location, an objective, what the crew sees at the Score, and the intel the crew can uncover by casing. The threats and the unknown obstacle are ST information: present only what’s visible, and let Planning reveal the rest.

Reading the Maps and Tables

  • One square = one spider. Dark brick is wall; grey blocks are furniture — climb it, or hide behind it for cover. Hatched squares are rough ground.
  • Gold letters are entry points. Red tokens are where threats start. Numbered circles are the Suggested Obstacles, in the order the crew meets them. Anything dashed in red is the unknown obstacle: leave it off the copy you show the crew.
  • E1 and E2 are the suggested Escape obstacles, along the dashed gold escape route. At least one of them never needs Athletics.
  • Difficulties are for a calm location (Chapter 15); the Alert raises them. Opposed means the creature rolls first, and its Successes + 1 are your Difficulty (Chapter 2). A heist obstacle takes two successful rolls; an Escape obstacle takes one (Chapter 11).

Heist 1 — The Cookie Situation House · Easy

Alert Limit: 10 · Loot: Crumb/Trinket · For: a first session

The Objective: a specific cookie — the good kind, from the tin with the blue lid — in the kitchen of a single-story house. In and out. Except the cat is new.

What the crew sees at the Score: a standard two-bedroom home. The objective is a tin on the second shelf of the cabinet just past the end of the counter. In plain sight: a young cat, very interested in things, and a human who gets up for water during the night. Two ways in: the wall vent, or up through the sink’s drain.

Intel the crew can uncover (Casing): the counter and the cabinet were wiped down this evening and are slippery (Acrobatics to climb). The cat’s bed is 9 squares from the cabinet. The human’s water runs roughly every ninety minutes. The bottle cap on the floor is exactly the kind of shiny thing the cat can’t ignore.

ST Prep — the human: asleep down the hall (a Sleeping Human, Chapter 15). A heist doesn’t last ninety minutes, so the next water run only comes early: when the Alert reaches 7, or at once on a Mid-Heist Complication of 3 (Chapter 20). They take 2 rounds to reach the sink, fill a glass for 1, and go back to bed. While they’re in the kitchen the lights are on, and Stealth against them is Difficulty 2.

ST Prep — the unknown obstacle: there are two identical tins on that shelf. One holds cookies. One holds sewing supplies. The crew finds out which the hard way. Before play, roll a d6 in secret: 1–3, the cookies are in the left tin; 4–6, the right. From outside, only a nose can tell them apart.

Heist 2 — The Office After Hours Office · Standard

Alert Limit: 8 · Loot: Prize · For: a second or third session

The Objective: a memory stick in the desk drawer of the corner office. Empty after 6 p.m. except for a cleaning crew that comes through at 8.

What the crew sees at the Score: a small open-plan office with a corner room for the manager, two floors, entry from the ground-floor ventilation. In plain sight: a motion-sensor light system across the main floor, and a guard spider working the overnight shift. The second way in is the gap under the service door.

Intel the crew can uncover (Casing): the two sensor beams each cover a known line of squares with a fixed timing gap. The guard spider’s patrol loops the perimeter every few rounds. The drawer uses a standard lock. The cable riser comes up behind the manager’s desk.

ST Prep — the guard spider: it knows someone’s inside, not where. Until it spots the crew (Chapter 16), it walks its loop of the upper floor from the stair landing: 28 squares, about 5 rounds at Speed 6, past the office door. Roll at the end of each lap (Chapter 14); on a 5–6, it takes the stairs down and walks the ground floor’s east end.

ST Prep — the unknown obstacle: the cleaning crew came at 7 tonight, not 8. They’re already here — upstairs, doing the corner office: a cleaner vacuuming inside with headphones on, the cart wedged in the doorway. They finish 6 rounds after the crew reaches the upper floor, then take the stairs down, park the cart across the vent, and prop the service door open for their bags.

First real opposed roll.Stealth, opposed (its Perception 4) · fight: Brawl, opposed (its Brawl 3) — fights are loud · talk: Persuasion (4), not the Face · bribe: its Weakness (Chapter 16)

E2The cleaning cart is parked across the vent the crew planned to leave through. Out the propped service door instead, past the cleaners.Past the cleaners: Stealth (1) · time the vacuum: Tactics (2) · shove the cart: Athletics (3), +1 Alert

Heist 3 — The Pet Store Problem Pet Store · Standard

Alert Limit: 8 · Loot: Prize · For: any session that wants guaranteed disaster

The Objective: a specific cricket — yes, a cricket — in the feeder bin behind the service counter. A client is paying in premium silk. Don’t ask.

What the crew sees at the Score: a three-aisle, single-floor retail space. In plain sight: a large corn snake in a display tank near the counter (its lid is loose, everyone knows, nobody has fixed it), and an alert parrot near the front that repeats what it hears, loudly and at length. Two ways in: the gap in the loading-dock door, or under the front door, right by the parrot.

Intel the crew can uncover (Casing): the snake is slow to commit but fast once it does. The parrot’s cage is 5 squares off the main aisle. The feeder bin sits behind the counter with one narrow approach. The parrot’s cover is folded on the shelf beside its cage, and there’s a box of crackers on the counter. The snake’s heat lamp is off for the night.

ST Prep — the unknown obstacle: there are forty-seven crickets in that bin. The client described the target as “the big one.” They are all the same size. This becomes a negotiation with a cricket. The crickets decide as a group: win them over and they push one forward. It is the big one. Nobody can prove otherwise.

Heist 4 — The Library Job Library · Hard

Alert Limit: 6 · Loot: Treasure · For: an experienced crew

The Objective: a rare book in the locked reference section of a public library. It contains a spider map — a generational silk record of every web in the city. Someone wants it back. Someone else wants it more.

What the crew sees at the Score: three floors; the reference section is on the second, behind a locked glass case. In plain sight: a head librarian who stays late and is extraordinarily observant, and the library cat — enormous, and it has seen things. Two ways in on the ground floor: the book-return slot in the front door, or the gap under the staff door.

Intel the crew can uncover (Casing): the librarian’s route covers every floor on an irregular schedule. The case uses an antique lock (Engineering, Difficulty 4). There are two stairwells and a dumbwaiter between floors. The dumbwaiter still runs, worked by a pulley inside the shaft.

ST Prep — the librarian: an Alert Human (Chapter 15). Roll 1d6 as each obstacle begins, and at the end of each round in turn order: 1–2, the ground floor; 3–4, the second floor; 5–6, the third floor, off the map. On a floor, they walk its centre aisle from one stairwell to the other and back. The library cat is a House Cat (Chapter 16), asleep on the second-floor landing; it wakes on its Escalation.

ST Prep — the unknown obstacle: another crew is already inside, after the same book. Four spiders built like the players’, rolling 5 dice at whatever they do (Attribute 3 + Skill 2). They got here first, found the antique lock beyond them, and are hiding in the reference room, waiting for someone else to open the case. When it opens, they step out.

E1Down two floors with a book that outweighs the crew.The dumbwaiter: Engineering (3) · the stairs: Athletics (3)
E2The lobby: the guard’s web on one side, maybe the librarian on the other.Stealth or Deception (4) · feed the book out through the return slot and follow it: Engineering (3)

Heist 5 — The Restaurant Rush Restaurant · Hard

Alert Limit: 6 · Loot: Treasure · For: any session wanting maximum disaster

The Objective: the chef’s personal recipe notebook, kept in the back office. A rival baker — a wasp, and nobody is happy about it — hired the crew. The place closes at 11; the crew moves at midnight. Except dinner service ran late.

What the crew sees at the Score: a mid-sized restaurant — kitchen, dining room, bar, small office. In plain sight: two closing staff finishing cleanup, and a kitchen that is still warm and smells extraordinary (a spider with Easily Distracted may have it triggered on entry). Two ways in: the alley vent over the bar, or under the kitchen’s back door.

Intel the crew can uncover (Casing): the staff move unpredictably with no set route. A rat lives under the dishwasher and runs protection for the block — he’ll want something. The office door sticks. The snap trap by the back door is new this week; it caught the rat’s cousin. The kitchen floor was mopped at closing.

ST Prep — the staff: two closing staff, Alert Humans, starting in the dining room. At the end of each round — outside turn order, each exchange of crew actions (Chapter 9) — roll 1d6 for each: 1–2, the dining room; 3–4, the kitchen; 5, the corridor; 6, a trash run out the back door, which stays propped open until the next 6.

ST Prep — the rat’s deal: he wants the trap gone, and to be treated like a professional. The crew gets one pitch: Persuasion, opposed by his Haggle 4; offering the trap, food or a favour owed is −1 Difficulty. On a Success, he grants safe passage and stays out of it. On a Partial, the deal holds, but the trap comes first (jamming it is Engineering 3). On a Failure there’s no deal: he blocks the way out of the kitchen, and getting past him is a fight. The deal goes bad the moment there’s a fight, or if the crew leaves with the trap still set: the Alert rises by 2, once, and he fights (Brawl 4).

ST Prep — the unknown obstacle: a third staff member fell asleep in the break room. They wake the first time the crew makes noise within 4 squares of the break room — any Partial or Failure there, a sneeze, the sticking office door — or when the Alert reaches 5 (Chapter 15: they stir). Awake, they’re an Alert Human, and they head straight for the office: their phone is on the desk.
