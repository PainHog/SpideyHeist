Chapter 18

The Alert in Play

Keep It Visible

The Alert should be physically present — a shared number, a row of coins, a line on the whiteboard. The crew should see it at all times. When it moves, say so, out loud. Let them feel it climb.

Never Apologize for Raising It

When the Alert goes up, it goes up. Don’t soften it. “That failed — Alert goes to 6” beats “sorry, the Alert goes to 6.” The meter climbing isn’t punishment; it’s the world reacting to what the crew is doing. Be honest about it.

Describe the Thresholds

When the Alert crosses a threshold, describe the change instead of naming the number. Not “the Alert hit 5” — “the cat opens its eyes. You can see it from here.” Same information, three times the weight.

The Limit Is a Moment, Not a Game-Over Screen

When the Alert hits the Limit mid-obstacle, finish the current action or round first, then bring down the lockdown — don’t cut a player off mid-roll. The objective may be gone, but the crew isn’t done. The escape is now a desperate sprint through a fully awake location. Play that. Give it weight. Make getting out feel earned.
