Chapter 12

Silk, Gadgets & Found Materials

Spiders don’t carry briefcases. They have three things: silk, whatever’s lying around the location, and a Tinkerer who can turn a bent paperclip into a grappling hook given forty minutes and no supervision.

Silk Is the Universal Tool

Every spider produces silk, and silk is always available. As your Action (or for 1 SP to skip the roll), you can run a Silk Line between two points up to 5 squares apart. A Silk Line is safe footing the crew can climb or cross with no Acrobatics roll — up a wall, across a gap, along a ceiling — and it holds up to two spiders at once. It stays until it’s broken or the scene ends.

Silk also does the small stuff without ceremony: anchoring yourself before a drop, tying off a light object to haul it, lowering a crewmate. If it’s the kind of thing silk obviously does and there’s no real risk, just do it.

Certain species and Roles push silk further — the Orb Weaver’s webs (Chapter 4), the Bruiser’s Silk Grapple, the Ghost’s Silk Trail. Those are on their entries.

Found Materials

The location is full of things a spider can use — a bottle cap, a rubber band, a paperclip, a coin balanced on an edge. Any spider can try to repurpose a found object with a WIT + Engineering roll; the Difficulty scales with how ambitious the idea is. Turning a bottle cap into a shield is Easy. Turning three rubber bands and a thumbtack into a working latch-release is not.

The Tinkerer’s “I Made a Thing” (Chapter 5) is the specialist version of this — faster, more reliable, and built for the moment you need a gadget now. Found materials are what everyone else uses when the Tinkerer is busy or in a jar.
